Blackpolis: mapping structural racism in Cali with census data

A policy brief addressed to Cali's equity officials. Using the 2018 census for 328 neighbourhoods, I measured how the share of Afro-Colombian residents relates to unemployment, schooling and illiteracy, then grouped neighbourhoods so that a programme could be targeted where the gaps are largest.

Data
DANE 2018 census · CaliEnDatos
Unit
328 neighbourhoods
Methods
OLS · clustering · QGIS

What the census shows

For each neighbourhood I took the share of residents who identify as Black, Afro-Colombian, Raizal or Palenquero (NARP) and regressed four outcomes on it. Every slope is significant at p < 0.001:

  • Unemployment: +0.30 per point of Afro share (R² 0.34).
  • School attendance, ages 5 to 21: −0.55 (R² 0.15).
  • Higher-education attendance: −1.09 (R² 0.33).
  • Illiteracy: +0.11 (R² 0.35).

Three kinds of neighbourhood

Clustering split the city into three groups. In the most disadvantaged one, 21% of residents are Afro-Colombian, unemployment is 24% and 13% attend higher education. In the most advantaged, 6% are Afro-Colombian and 54.7% attend higher education. The maps, made in QGIS, show the clusters as contiguous areas of the city.

Recommendation

I scored three options against the same criteria: the status quo (18 points), an education programme (21) and a labour programme (23). The brief recommends labour-market entry and personal-finance programmes, targeted at the neighbourhoods in the disadvantaged cluster.

The analysis is in Python (pandas, scikit-learn, plotly): blackpolis_replication on GitHub.
