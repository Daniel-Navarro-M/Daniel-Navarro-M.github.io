Parental investment and learning gaps in the Western Balkans

The World Bank's education team for the Western Balkans asked a group of four CEU students a question: how much does family background shape a child's math score in fourth grade, and does the gap look different in the Balkans than in the rest of Europe? I led the data work.

Client
World Bank, Vienna office
Data
TIMSS Grade 4 · ~90k students a wave
Status
Unpublished, internal paper

The question

The focus countries were Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Serbia, compared with the rest of Europe. We looked at two things: the score gap between children of more and less educated parents, and parental investment, meaning the early learning activities parents report doing with their child at home before school.

Data

TIMSS Grade 4 math for 2011, 2015, 2019 and 2023, covering about 90,000 students per wave in the countries we studied (90,861 in 2019 and 90,212 in 2023). The factor model uses 311,597 observations. For the dynamic part we used the TIMSS 2023 longitudinal sample, which follows students from Grade 4 to Grade 5 in Sweden, Italy, Slovenia, Kosovo, North Macedonia and Montenegro.

Doing survey statistics properly with TIMSS

TIMSS is a complex survey. Ignoring its design gives standard errors that are far too small, so every estimate uses:

  • Jackknife replication (JK2) with 75 sampling zones and 150 replicate weights.
  • Five plausible values per student, combined with Rubin's rules.
  • A common outcome scale: each student's percentile rank in math across Europe, which makes countries comparable.
  • A latent measure of parental investment: a confirmatory factor model on the ordered home-activity items, estimated with WLSMV.

To check the parental-education measure, I compared its country averages with World Bank data on tertiary attainment. The work is in R (survey, fixest), with the IEA IDB Analyzer for descriptives, two-way fixed effects on the longitudinal panel, and QGIS for the maps.

Results: unpublished

The findings belong to an internal World Bank policy paper and are not public. This page covers the question, data and methods only.

Team and deliverable

I worked with Róisín Downes, Taneesha Shekhawat and Unyime-Young Mfon, under CEU faculty supervision, from September 2025 to March 2026. The output was an internal policy paper for the World Bank, plus a poster. The analysis code is on GitHub: World Bank CEU TIMSS analysis.
